    Chapter 0

    Start here: what AATP is

    AATP answers one question every time a customer orders: how much can we promise, and by what date?

    ATP and AATP

    ATP means Available-to-Promise. It is the check SAP runs when you save a sales order. It looks at stock and expected receipts and says "yes, 600 pieces on Monday".

    AATP (Advanced ATP) is the newer version inside S/4HANA. It does the same check faster, and adds business rules on top: who should get stock first, how much each region may take, what to do when the plant is short.

    Question from the businessClassic ATP (same as ECC)AATP
    Is stock available for this order?YesYes, faster
    Can each region have a fixed share of short stock?Old SD product allocation, hard to maintainProduct Allocation
    Can we keep some stock aside for one channel?NoSupply Protection
    Can important orders take stock from less important ones?Rescheduling (V_V2), first come first servedBackorder Processing with priorities
    Can the system offer another plant or product by itself?NoAlternative-Based Confirmation
    Can we tie exact stock to an exact order?NoSupply Assignment
    Can we promise based on a real production plan?NoSupply Creation-Based Confirmation

    The parts of AATP

    AATP is not one feature. It is a family. This course has one chapter for each part.

    PartShort nameWhat it does in one line

    Product AllocationPALSets a maximum each group may take

    How AATP is switched on

    1. Every product has a checking group on its master record (field "Availability check").
    2. In the checking group setup (transaction OVZ2, "Define Checking Groups") there is a field Advanced ATP. Set it to Active.
    3. All products with that checking group now use AATP. Products with other checking groups stay on classic ATP.

    So you can start small: create one new checking group, switch AATP on for it, and give it only to the products that need it.

    Check the licence first On S/4HANA on-premise and private cloud, AATP must be activated and needs an additional licence from SAP. On public cloud, AATP is the only version; basic features are in the standard licence and the advanced ones need the extra licence. Ask the client's SAP account manager before you design anything.
    One side effect Once a product uses AATP, the old rescheduling transactions (V_V2, V_RA, CO06) no longer work for it. You must set up Backorder Processing (Chapter 4) to replace them.

    Two things every part shares

    Characteristics
    These are order fields you use to write rules, such as sales organisation, region, customer group, customer or product. Allocation, protection, alternatives and backorder rules all use them. You choose which fields are available in the characteristic catalog apps.
    Review Availability Check Result (app F4719)
    This screen explains a confirmation step by step: what availability gave, what allocation gave, what protection held back. Use it whenever a user asks "why did I get only this much?"

    The example used in this course

    All scenarios use an innerwear company. It makes vests and briefs, stores them in depots (Chennai, Bengaluru, Delhi) and sells to distributors (general trade), big retail chains (modern trade) and e-commerce sites.

    Chapter 1

    Product Availability Check

    The base of everything. It decides how much stock is free to promise, and on which date.

    Business scenario A distributor orders 800 vests today. The plant has 600 in stock. Production will bring 400 more on day 5. Another customer already holds a confirmed order for 300, to be delivered on day 3. How much can we promise the new order?

    The basic sum

    Free to promise = stock + expected receipts − already promised

    • Stock: what is physically in the plant.
    • Expected receipts: production orders, purchase orders, planned orders.
    • Already promised: other sales orders, deliveries, reservations.

    Stock promised to someone else is gone, even if their delivery is a few days away.

    The three settings that control the check

    1. Checking group (on the product)
    Says how this product is checked, and whether AATP is on for it.
    2. Checking rule (from the transaction)
    A sales order uses rule A. A delivery uses rule B. So the same product can be checked one way at order time and another way at delivery time.
    3. Scope of check (checking group + checking rule, transaction OVZ9)
    A list of tick-boxes that says what to count.
    Group of tick-boxesExamplesThink about
    Which stockSafety stock, quality inspection stock, blocked stock, stock in transitQuality stock may fail. Counting it means you may promise goods you cannot ship.
    Which receiptsPurchase orders, production orders, planned orders, purchase requisitionsA released production order is reliable. A planned order is only a plan.
    Which issuesSales orders, deliveries, reservations, dependent requirementsIf you leave sales orders out, the same stock gets promised twice.
    Replenishment lead timeOn or offSee below. This one causes the most trouble.

    Replenishment lead time

    This is the total time to make or buy the product, say 20 days.

    • Switched on: the system thinks "after 20 days I can always get more". It confirms any quantity at day 20, even when nothing is planned.
    • Switched off: the system confirms only against real stock and real receipts.
    Common complaint "The order was confirmed in full, but on the date there was no stock." Nine times out of ten, replenishment lead time is on and nobody planned production. For products that are often short, switch it off.

    How the date is worked out

    The system first works backward from the date the customer wants:

    Wanted delivery date − transit time − loading time − pick and pack time = date stock must be ready

    If stock is ready on that date, the wanted date is confirmed. If not, the system finds the first date stock is ready and works forward, adding the same times back.

    Transit time comes from the route. Loading time and pick and pack time come from the shipping point.

    What the user sees when stock is short

    • One-time delivery: take what is available on the wanted date, nothing more.
    • Complete delivery: everything together, on the first date the full quantity is available.
    • Delivery proposal: part deliveries, as stock becomes available.

    Which choices are allowed depends on the partial delivery agreement in the customer master.

    What AATP adds to this check

    • It is much faster on large order volumes.
    • Many users can check the same product at the same moment. The system holds a small temporary reservation for each user while they work, so nobody blocks anybody.
    • The result screen explains itself (Review Availability Check Result).

    Setup steps

    1. Define the checking group and set Advanced ATP to Active (OVZ2).
    2. Put the checking group on the product (MRP 3 view or Sales: General/Plant view).
    3. Set the scope of check for the checking group with rule A (order) and rule B (delivery) (OVZ9).
    4. Check that the requirement class and the schedule line category have both "availability check" and "transfer of requirements" switched on.
    5. Maintain transit time on routes and loading and pick and pack time on shipping points.
    6. Test with a few orders and read the result in Review Availability Check Result.

    Chapter 2

    Product Allocation

    A quota. It sets the most that each region, channel or customer may take in a period, even when stock is there.

    Business scenario Before Diwali, a new premium brief is in high demand. The factory can make 50,000 pieces a month. On day one, a big distributor in the North orders 40,000. If the system confirms it, the South, West and East get almost nothing, and those distributors move to a competitor.

    The idea

    You decide in advance how the month's supply is shared. The availability check may say "50,000 available", but the allocation check says "North may take 15,000 this month". The order gets the lower of the two.

    The building blocks

    Product allocation object
    The container for one quota plan, for example "Premium brief, by region". It holds the characteristics and the time period type (day, week or month).
    Characteristics
    What the quota is split by: sales organisation, region, customer group, customer, and so on. You can combine them, for example region and channel.
    Planning data
    The actual numbers: 15,000 for North in October, 15,000 in November. Entered in an app or uploaded from a file.
    Assignment
    Which product in which plant uses this object, and from which date to which date.
    Sequence
    Lets one product use more than one object. See below.

    Using quota from other periods

    • Backward consumption: if this month's quota is finished, the system may use quota left over from earlier months.
    • Forward consumption: the system may use next month's quota. The confirmed date then moves into next month.
    • Neither: each month stands alone. Unused quota is lost at month end.

    You set how many periods back and forward are allowed.

    Sequences: alternatives and constraints

    Alternatives (OR)
    "Try the customer's own quota first. If it is finished, use the region quota." The order may draw from one pot after another.
    Constraints (AND)
    "The order must fit in the region quota and in the channel quota." The order gets the smallest result of all the pots.

    Worth knowing

    • The quota is checked against a date. You choose whether that is the date stock must be ready or the wanted delivery date.
    • It works for sales orders and for stock transfer orders.
    • If stock itself is less than the quota, the order gets only the stock. Quota is a ceiling, not a guarantee.
    • Switching on AATP product allocation is a one-way step. You cannot go back to the old SD product allocation afterwards.

    Setup steps (apps)

    1. Activate product allocation in customising (once per client).
    2. Configure Product Allocation (F2119): create the object, pick the characteristics and period type.
    3. Manage Product Allocation Planning Data (F2121): enter the quantities for each value and period.
    4. Manage Product Allocation Sequences: only if you need alternatives or constraints.
    5. Assign Product to Product Allocation (F2120): link product and plant, with validity dates.
    6. Activate the object and test. Product Allocation Overview shows how much of each quota is used.
    Common mistakes
    • Quota left in place after the shortage is over. Stock sits in the warehouse while orders stay unconfirmed. Give every allocation an end date.
    • A customer or region with no planning data gets zero. Decide what "everyone else" should receive and plan it.
    • Too many characteristics. Region by channel by customer by week is a lot of numbers for someone to maintain. Start simple.

    Chapter 3

    Supply Protection

    The opposite of a quota. It keeps a minimum quantity aside for one group, so that other orders cannot use it.

    Business scenario The plant has 10,000 pieces. Your contract with an e-commerce site says you must always be able to ship 2,000 a week, or you pay a penalty. General trade distributors order early in the week and usually take everything.

    The idea

    You tell the system "2,000 pieces of this product in this plant are protected for e-commerce". When any other customer orders, the availability check behaves as if those 2,000 are not there.

    How it behaves

    • When the protected group orders, its protected quantity is used first. It can also take open stock if there is any.
    • Every other group sees: free stock minus what is protected for others.
    • Protection is planned per time period, for example per week. You can protect more in festival weeks and less afterwards.
    • When a period ends, protection that was not used expires. That stock becomes free for everyone.
    • It works for sales orders and stock transfer orders, and the backorder run respects it.

    Quota or protection? The difference

    Product AllocationSupply Protection
    What it setsA maximum a group may takeA minimum kept for a group
    Business sentence"North must not take more than 15,000""E-commerce must always find 2,000"
    Who is limitedThe group itselfEveryone else
    Based onPlanned numbers, separate from stockReal supply of a product in a plant
    Typical useFair sharing during a shortageContracts, penalties, key channels

    You can use both together. Protection lowers what availability returns. Allocation then caps the result.

    The building blocks

    Supply protection object
    Created for one product in one plant.
    Protection groups
    Who is protected, described with characteristics. Example: distribution channel = e-commerce. One object can hold several groups.
    Protected quantity per period
    The number to hold back in each week or month.
    Priority levels
    If supply is too small to protect everybody, a prioritised characteristic decides which group is served first.

    Setup steps

    1. Make sure the fields you need (channel, customer group and so on) are available as characteristics.
    2. Manage Supply Protection (F4569): create the object for product and plant.
    3. Add the protection groups and their values.
    4. Enter the protected quantity for each period.
    5. Activate, then test with one order from inside the group and one from outside.
    Common mistakes
    • Protecting too much. Stock sits idle while other customers stay unconfirmed. Review the numbers every planning cycle.

    Chapter 4

    Backorder Processing

    A job that looks at all open orders together and re-shares the confirmations by business priority.

    Business scenario Yesterday a small retailer's order took 300 of the last pieces. Today a key modern trade customer orders 400 of the same item, urgently. Also, one customer with confirmed stock has just gone on credit block. Nothing changes by itself: the urgent order shows zero.

    Why it is needed

    SAP confirms orders first come, first served. After an order is saved, the system never rechecks it on its own. So confirmations go out of date when:

    • an important order arrives late,
    • production comes early or late,
    • an order is cancelled or blocked and its stock should go to someone else.

    Backorder processing fixes this. It usually runs every night.

    The building blocks

    Segment
    A named group of order lines, chosen by a filter, with a sort order inside it. Example: "Modern trade orders due this week, oldest first".
    Confirmation strategy
    How a segment is treated in the run. There are seven (table below).
    Variant
    The full recipe for one run: which segments, and which strategy for each.
    Run
    The job that executes a variant. It can run for real or as a simulation that changes nothing.

    The seven strategies

    StrategyWhat happens to these ordersTypical use
    WinMust be confirmed in full on the wanted date. If that is impossible, the run reports a problem.Rush orders, top key accounts
    GainKeep at least what they have. Get more if possible. If they cannot keep it, the run reports a problem.Priority customers with orders due soon
    ImproveTry to keep and improve, but may lose if higher groups need the stock. No problem is reported. Added in release 2022.Good customers you would like to help, without a hard promise
    RedistributeMay end up better, the same or worse.All normal orders
    FillNever get more. Keep what they have, or lose some.Low priority orders, internal stock transfers
    LoseLose all confirmation.Credit-blocked customers
    SkipLeft out of the run. Stay exactly as they are.Orders you do not want touched

    The run serves Win first, then Gain, then the middle groups, then Fill. Stock freed from Lose orders goes to the groups above.

    Sorting inside a segment

    When two orders in the same segment compete, the sort order decides. Common choices: wanted delivery date, order creation date, delivery priority from the customer master. If the standard fields are not enough, the app Configure Custom BOP Sorting lets you define your own order of values.

    Setup steps (apps)

    1. Agree the priority rules with the business first. This is the hard part.
    2. Configure BOP Segment (F2158): create one segment for each group, with filter and sort.
    3. Configure BOP Variant (F2160): put the segments under the right strategies.
    4. Schedule BOP Run (F2665): run it in simulation first, then schedule the real job.
    5. Monitor BOP Run (F2159): see which orders gained, which lost, and any problems.

    When to run it

    After the night's planning run and goods receipts, and before delivery creation in the morning. Some businesses add a second run at midday.

    Common mistakes
    • Putting too many orders in Win. If everything is top priority, nothing is.
    • Forgetting the customer side. An order that loses its confirmation may already have been promised by phone. Agree who informs the customer.
    • No simulation before go-live. Always simulate and read the monitor with the sales head.

    Chapter 5

    Alternative-Based Confirmation

    When the asked plant cannot deliver in full and on time, the system looks at other plants, or at a replacement product, by itself.

    Business scenario A customer near Chennai orders 2,000 pieces. The Chennai depot has 500. The Bengaluru depot has 3,000. Today the order desk phones around, then changes the plant in the order by hand. Sometimes nobody notices and the customer waits two weeks.

    The idea

    For orders you choose, the system checks a list of alternative plants at the same moment as the normal check, compares the results, and confirms from the best one.

    Two kinds of alternative

    Plant substitution
    Same product, different plant or depot.
    Product substitution
    Different product, for example the new pack that replaces an old one, or the same vest under a new article number. Which product may replace which is kept as substitution master data.

    Newer releases can combine both: another product from another plant.

    The two decisions you configure

    1. Who gets alternatives (alternative control)
    Not every order should be allowed to switch plant. You use characteristics to say which ones may: for example only modern trade customers, or only certain product groups.
    2. How to choose between alternatives (substitution strategy)
    The rule for picking a winner. Examples: prefer the asked plant if it can deliver in full; otherwise prefer the plant that can deliver everything on time; otherwise split across as few plants as possible.

    What the user sees

    • If the whole line moves, the plant on the order line changes.
    • If the line is split, the system creates sub-items under the main line, one for each plant, each with its own quantity and date.
    • Every alternative is checked fully: availability, protection and allocation all apply in the other plant too.
    Check these before you switch it on in India
    • GST: the delivering plant decides the tax. Chennai to a Tamil Nadu customer is CGST and SGST. Bengaluru to the same customer is IGST. Pricing and invoicing must handle both.
    • Freight: a farther depot costs more. Decide who pays.
    • Master data: the product must be set up in every alternative plant, with shipping point and route determination working from there.
    • Customer rules: some customers accept only one delivery, or only from one depot.

    Setup steps (apps)

    1. List which plants may stand in for which, and for which customers. Agree it with sales and logistics.
    2. Configure Substitution Strategy (F2699): define how alternatives are found and ranked.
    3. Configure Alternative Control (F2698): say which orders use which strategy, by characteristic values.
    4. Newer releases also offer Manage Characteristic Combinations (F5303) and Configure Alternative Determination (F5354) for finer rules.
    5. Test with orders that should switch and orders that should not.

    App names and options differ a little between releases. Check what your system has.

    Chapter 6

    Supply Assignment and Release for Delivery

    Confirmation says "you will get 400". Assignment says "these exact 400 pieces are yours". Release decides when the order is complete enough to ship.

    Business scenario Distributors book the winter thermal range three months ahead, before it is made. One order has four sizes: 80, 85, 90 and 95, 100 pieces each. Sizes 80 and 85 are in the warehouse. Size 90 comes from production in six days. Size 95 is not planned yet. The warehouse ships the two sizes it has. The distributor is unhappy: half a size range does not sell.

    Confirmation and assignment are different things

    Availability check (confirmation)Supply assignment
    Looks atThe total pool: all stock and receipts against all ordersOne order line and one specific supply
    ResultA quantity and a dateA fixed link: this stock or this production order belongs to this order line
    How long it holdsUntil someone rechecksUntil the delivery is made, unless you remove it

    Supply assignment came from SAP's fashion solutions, where it was called the allocation run. That is why its short name is still ARun.

    How it works

    1. The product is marked as relevant for supply assignment in the plant (a field on the product master).
    2. A supply assignment rule says how supply and orders are matched: which supply types may be used and in what order.
    3. The assignment runs as part of a backorder run. So the same segments and priorities from Chapter 4 decide who is served first.
    4. Each order line gets a status. Linked to stock in the warehouse: fixed, ready for delivery. Linked to a future receipt such as a purchase or production order: reserved. Put aside for now: on hold.
    5. An optional release check looks at how complete the order is. A release rule sets two percentages, one for the whole order and one for each line, for example 90% and 80%. Only orders that pass get a delivery.

    Apps

    • Configure Supply Assignment Rules: the matching rules.
    • Monitor Supply Assignment Runs (F3684) and Monitor Supply Assignment Demand: what the run did.
    • Supply Demand Overview: supply and demand side by side, with what is assigned.
    • Adjust Supply Assignment (also called Insight to Action, transaction ARUNITA): assign, unassign, release or block by hand.
    • Maintain Release Rules: the percentages for the release check.
    Scope note Supply assignment belongs to the fashion and vertical business part of S/4HANA. Before proposing it, check that the client's system and licence include it, and that the products are set up for it.

    Release for Delivery

    This is a separate, simpler tool for the last step before shipping. It does not need supply assignment.

    Business scenario At 5 pm the dispatch manager learns that a key customer's truck leaves tonight. His order is short by 200. Another order with 200 confirmed is not leaving until next week.
    • The Release for Delivery app (F1786) shows the manager all orders that are due, with wanted and confirmed quantities.
    • He can move confirmed quantity from one order to another on the screen and see the effect at once.
    • When he is satisfied, he releases, and deliveries can be created for those orders.
    • Configure Order Fulfillment Responsibilities (F2246) decides which products and plants each manager sees.

    Think of backorder processing as the automatic nightly decision, and Release for Delivery as the human correction during the day.

    Supply Creation-Based Confirmation

    When stock and planned receipts are not enough, the system plans new production on the spot and confirms the order from that plan.

    Business scenario An institutional buyer orders 1,000 custom-printed vests. Stock is 600. Nothing more is planned. The sales person wants to give a delivery date he can trust for the other 400 while the customer is still on the phone.

    The idea

    The availability check finds the gap. It then asks the production planning tool (PP/DS, the detailed scheduling part of S/4HANA) a real question: "Can you make 400, and by when?" PP/DS looks at free machine time and at components, and creates a planned order. The sales order is confirmed against that planned order.

    • It arrived with the 2022 release. It replaces the older "capable-to-promise" function from SAP APO.

    • It must be activated in customising, and PP/DS must be set up for the product.

    Chapter 8

    Putting it together

    How the parts combine on one order, which part answers which business problem, and how to run an AATP project.

    One order, all the checks

    Business scenario Diwali week. The Delhi depot has 10,000 premium briefs. 1,000 are protected for e-commerce. The North region has 3,000 left in this month's quota. A North general trade distributor orders 5,000.

    What can happen to the open 2,000:

    • If alternatives are allowed, the system tries another depot. The quota still applies there, so in this case it would not help.
    • If forward consumption is allowed, the system uses next month's North quota and confirms a date next month.
    • If neither, the 2,000 stay unconfirmed. A later backorder run will confirm them when quota or priority allows.

    Which part for which problem

    The business saysUse
    "Orders are confirmed but stock is never there."Availability check: review scope of check and replenishment lead time
    "One big customer takes everything."Product Allocation
    "This channel must always find stock."Supply Protection
    "Important orders that come late get nothing."Backorder Processing
    "Stock is in the next depot but the order shows short."Alternative-Based Confirmation
    "We ship half size ranges." / "Pre-booked orders lose their stock."Supply Assignment with release check
    "Dispatch needs to shift stock between orders at the last minute."Release for Delivery
    "We need a trustworthy date for made-to-order items."Supply Creation-Based Confirmation

    How to run an AATP project

    1. Licence and scope. Confirm the AATP licence. For supply assignment and supply creation, confirm those too.
    2. Pick the products. Start with the products that are often short. Give them a new checking group with AATP active.
    3. Fix the basics. Clean scope of check, lead times, routes and shipping point times. Advanced features cannot repair a wrong basic check.
    4. Get the priority rules in writing. Who is a key account? Which channel comes first? Who decides quotas each month? This is a business decision, not an SAP one.
    5. Build in this order: availability check, backorder processing, then allocation or protection, then alternatives. Each step is useful by itself.
    6. Simulate. Run backorder processing in simulation with real orders and walk through the monitor with the sales head.
    7. Name the owners. Someone must maintain quotas and protected quantities every cycle and read the backorder monitor every morning.
    8. Train the order desk to read Review Availability Check Result, so they can explain a confirmation to a customer.
    What goes wrong most often
    • Old quotas and protections left active after the shortage ends.
    • Rules too detailed for anyone to maintain.
    • Nobody tells customers when the backorder run takes their confirmation away.
    • Plant substitution switched on without checking GST and freight.
    • Old rescheduling jobs left running, doing nothing for AATP products.

    Project kit

    What to ask the client, what to decide, how to cut over and what to tick off before go-live.

    Workshop questions for the client

    Today's situation
    • Which products are regularly short? In which months?
    • How do you decide today who gets short stock? Who makes that call?
    • How often are orders confirmed and then not delivered on the date? Do you measure it?
    • Do you run any rescheduling job today? Does anyone trust its result?
    • How do customers learn that a date has moved?
    Availability check
    • Should stock in quality inspection count as available? How often does it fail?
    • Should planned production count, or only released production orders?
    • Do you promise from a lead time when nothing is planned? Should you?
    • Are transit times per route and handling times per depot correct in the system?
    • Which customers accept part deliveries? Which insist on complete orders?
    • Is stock promised by depot, or by storage location inside the depot?
    Sharing rules: quota and protection
    • By what do you want to share: region, channel, customer group, single customers?
    • Is the rule "at most" for a group (quota) or "at least" for a group (protection)?
    • Monthly or weekly numbers? Who sets them, from which plan, and by which day?
    • May a group use quota it did not use last month? May it borrow from next month?
    • What should a customer outside all rules receive?
    • Do contracts with penalties exist for any channel? For what quantity and period?
    • When does the shortage end, and who switches the rules off?
    Priorities for the backorder run
    • Name the customer groups from most to least important. Is there a written list of key accounts?
    • Which orders must never lose a confirmation once given?
    • What happens to orders of credit-blocked customers?
    • Do stock transfers to depots rank above or below customer orders?
    • Inside one group, what decides: delivery date, order date, delivery priority?
    • When should the run happen, relative to planning, goods receipts and delivery creation?
    • Who reads the result each morning, and who informs customers who lost?
    Alternatives, assignment and release
    • Which depots may supply in place of which? For which customers?
    • Who pays extra freight? Is the GST difference between states handled in pricing and invoices?
    • Which products may replace which? Does the customer need to agree?
    • Do you pre-book orders before stock exists? Must their stock be held for them?
    • What share of an order must be ready before you ship it?
    • Does dispatch need to move stock between orders on the day? Who is allowed to?

    Cutover steps

    1. Transports. Move customising: checking group, scope of check, requirement class and schedule line settings, product allocation activation, item category entries.
    2. App content. Catalogs, allocation objects, sequences, protection objects, segments and variants are created in the apps. Check whether your release can transport or export them. If not, re-create them in production from a written sheet and have a second person check each one.
    3. Roles. Assign the business roles. Test that each named owner can open their apps.
    4. Stop the old jobs. Remove V_V2 and similar jobs for the products in scope.
    5. Load numbers. Upload quota planning data and protected quantities for the current and next periods. Nothing is carried over from old allocation data.
    6. Switch the products. Change the checking group on the products, by mass change, at a quiet time. Existing orders keep their old confirmations.
    7. First run in simulation. Run the backorder variant in simulation over all open orders. Review the monitor with the sales head.
    8. First real run. Run it for real. This brings all open orders under the new rules.
    9. Tell customers. Send the list of orders that lost confirmation to the people who will call customers.
    10. Schedule. Set up the recurring jobs.
    Fallback plan If results are wrong on day one, put the products back on the old checking group and stop the new jobs. Orders keep whatever confirmation they have. Product allocation activation itself cannot be reversed, so set all quota lines to unrestricted until the problem is fixed.

    Glossary

    ATP quantity
    Stock plus expected receipts minus what is already promised.
    Checking group
    Field on the product that says how it is checked and whether AATP is on.
    Checking rule
    Comes from the transaction: A for sales orders, B for deliveries.
    Scope of check
    The tick-boxes that say which stock, receipts and issues count.
    Replenishment lead time
    Days to make or buy the product. If on, anything is treated as available after that many days.
    Material availability date
    The date stock must be ready in the plant so that the delivery date can be met.
    Characteristic
    An order field used in rules: region, channel, customer group and so on.
    Product allocation object
    The container for one quota plan.
    Backward and forward consumption
    Using quota from earlier or later periods.
    Supply protection object
    Holds protected quantities for a product in a plant.
    Segment
    A filtered, sorted group of orders in backorder processing.
    Variant
    The recipe for one backorder run: segments and their strategies.
    Confirmation strategy
    Win, Gain, Improve, Redistribute, Fill, Lose or Skip.
    Alternative control
    Decides which orders may use another plant or product.
    Substitution strategy
    Decides how the system chooses between alternatives.
    Supply assignment (ARun)
    A fixed link between one supply and one order line.
    Release check
    Tests whether enough of an order is assigned before a delivery is allowed.
    PP/DS
    Production Planning and Detailed Scheduling, the planning tool used by supply creation-based confirmation.
